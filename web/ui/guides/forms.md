# Forms

The IRS forms the guides name (requirements S12.7.8), shown as the terms are (S12.7.7). Each
heading is a form and its title; the paragraph under it, a sentence or two of plain text, says
what it is for; a last line that is only a link is the form's page on irs.gov. A guide names a
form as "Form 8949" or "Schedule D" in full, or "1099-DIV" or "W-2" alone, since they can be
nothing else; a bare number ("8949") is never taken for a form. Kept in alphabetical order; a
test fails when a guide names a form missing here.

## Form 1040: U.S. Individual Income Tax Return

The yearly federal income tax return of a US citizen or resident. Its schedules, such as A and
D, and forms such as 8949 are filed with it.

[IRS: About Form 1040](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040)

## Form 1040-X: Amended U.S. Individual Income Tax Return

Corrects a Form 1040 already filed: each figure as filed, the change, and the corrected amount,
with why it changed.

[IRS: About Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040x)

## Form 1098: Mortgage Interest Statement

Sent by your mortgage lender early in the year: the interest you paid on the mortgage last year,
when it was $600 or more. It may be deductible on Schedule A.

[IRS: About Form 1098](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1098)

## Form 1098-E: Student Loan Interest Statement

Sent by a student loan's lender or servicer: the interest you paid on the loan in the year, when
it was $600 or more. Some of it may be deductible, within limits.

[IRS: About Form 1098-E](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1098-e)

## Form 1098-T: Tuition Statement

Sent by a college or other eligible school for each student: the qualified tuition and related
expenses paid in the year, and the scholarships and grants. It backs a claim to the education
credits.

[IRS: About Form 1098-T](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1098-t)

## Form 1099-B: Proceeds from Broker and Barter Exchange Transactions

Sent by your broker: each sale in a taxable account in the year, with its proceeds and, where
the broker knows it, its cost basis. The sales go on Form 8949.

[IRS: About Form 1099-B](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-b)

## Form 1099-DIV: Dividends and Distributions

Sent by a bank, broker or fund: the dividends and other distributions it paid you in the year,
ordinary and qualified dividends, capital-gain distributions and returns of capital.

[IRS: About Form 1099-DIV](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-div)

## Form 1099-INT: Interest Income

Sent by a bank, broker or other payer: the interest it paid you in the year, usually when it was
$10 or more.

[IRS: About Form 1099-INT](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-int)

## Form 1099-Q: Payments from Qualified Education Programs (Under Sections 529 and 530)

Sent by a 529 plan or Coverdell account for a year it paid out: the distributions, and how much
of them was earnings. The earnings not covered by qualified education expenses may be taxed.

[IRS: About Form 1099-Q](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-q)

## Form 709: United States Gift (and Generation-Skipping Transfer) Tax Return

The return for gifts to one person in a year above the annual exclusion, and for spreading a
large 529 contribution over five years. Tax is rarely due: the gifts count against a lifetime
exemption.

[IRS: About Form 709](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-709)

## Form 8283: Noncash Charitable Contributions

Filed with your return to deduct donated goods and other property worth more than $500 in the
year. An item worth more than $5,000 usually needs a qualified appraisal too.

[IRS: About Form 8283](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8283)

## Form 8606: Nondeductible IRAs

Records nondeductible contributions to a traditional IRA, the basis they build, and the taxable
part of IRA distributions and Roth conversions. The basis carries from year to year.

[IRS: About Form 8606](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8606)

## Form 8615: Tax for Certain Children Who Have Unearned Income

Figures the tax on a child's interest, dividends and gains above a yearly threshold at the
a parent's rate: the kiddie tax. It applies under age 18, and to some older children.

[IRS: About Form 8615](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8615)

## Form 8937: Report of Organizational Actions Affecting Basis of Securities

Published by a company after a split, spin-off, merger or similar action: how it changes the cost
basis of the shares you held.

[IRS: About Form 8937](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8937)

## Form 8949: Sales and Other Dispositions of Capital Assets

Lists each sale of shares, funds, bitcoin and other capital assets in the year: when it was
bought and sold, the proceeds, the basis and the gain or loss. Its totals go on Schedule D.

[IRS: About Form 8949](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8949)

## Form W-2: Wage and Tax Statement

Sent by your employer early in the year: the wages you were paid last year, and the income,
Social Security and Medicare taxes withheld from them.

[IRS: About Form W-2](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-2)

## Schedule A: Itemized Deductions

The part of Form 1040 that lists itemized deductions, such as mortgage interest, state and local
taxes, gifts to charity and large medical costs. It is used when they come to more than the
standard deduction.

[IRS: About Schedule A (Form 1040)](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-schedule-a-form-1040)

## Schedule D: Capital Gains and Losses

The part of Form 1040 that totals the year's capital gains and losses, short and long term, from
Form 8949 and capital-gain distributions, with last year's loss carryover.

[IRS: About Schedule D (Form 1040)](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-schedule-d-form-1040)
